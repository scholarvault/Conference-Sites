CONFERENCE AWARDS & RECOGNITION

GRAND PRIZE

Best Paper in AI Ethics & Governance

Awarded to the submission exhibiting the highest theoretical novelty, regulatory foresight, and empirical rigor in algorithmic fairness or AI compliance.

  • Certificate of Honor & Gold Plenary Laureate
  • Invited featured keynote presentation slot
OPEN SCIENCE PRIZE

Transparency & Reproducibility Award

Recognizes the research contribution accompanied by the most comprehensive, containerized, and independently verifiable code/data artifact repository.

  • Verified Artifact Laureate Badge
  • Featured highlight in conference proceedings
STAGE COMMENDATION

Best Virtual Research Presentation

Honors the presenter delivering the most articulate, pedagogically structured, and compelling virtual live presentation during plenary sessions.

  • Outstanding Orator Certificate
  • Audience choice & session chair evaluation
EARLY CAREER

Outstanding Young Researcher Award

Conferred upon promising PhD candidates or postdocs within 3 years of doctoral completion showing transformative leadership in responsible AI.

  • Young Scholar Laureate Commendation
  • Fast-track editorial mentorship pathway
FORENSICS

Forensic Detection Innovation Award

Recognizes novel algorithms or forensic tools that detect synthetic data falsification, paper-mill manipulation, or generative hallucination.

  • Integrity Innovation Certificate
  • Special recommendation for open-source adoption
SERVICE

Excellence in Peer-Review Service

Honors TPC members who provide the most rigorous, timely, constructive, and ethical reviews within the summit's 2–4 working day review SLA.

  • Distinguished Reviewer Laureate
  • Editorial Board recognition in proceedings
EVALUATION PROTOCOLS

Evaluation Criteria & Conflict of Interest

All accepted presentations are automatically screened for eligible award categories. A dedicated independent Awards Committee evaluates submissions based on double-blind review scores, methodology rigor, artifact validation, and live presentation clarity.

Strict Conflict-of-Interest (COI) firewalls prevent committee members from evaluating contributions from their own institutions, co-authors, or direct research collaborators.
